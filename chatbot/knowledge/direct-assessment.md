# Direct Assessment and the Informal Sector — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

Direct Assessment is the method by which the Akwa Ibom State Internal Revenue
Service (AKIRS) assesses the personal income tax of self-employed persons and
individuals in business, trade, profession or vocation, as distinct from
employees taxed under PAYE. Under Direct Assessment the taxpayer declares their
income, and AKIRS determines or confirms the tax due on that income. It applies
to sole proprietors, professionals in private practice, traders, artisans, and
other individuals who earn income outside formal employment, including the
informal sector.

## Who it applies to

Direct Assessment applies to any individual who earns income as gain or profit
from any trade, business, profession or vocation, for whatever period the trade,
business, profession or vocation is carried on. It also applies to operators in
the informal sector — businesses that are registered or unregistered and that do
not necessarily comply with formal legal or tax obligations. AKIRS has a
dedicated Directorate of Informal Sector responsible for collecting taxes from
such businesses.

## What a taxpayer must declare

A self-employed taxpayer should register with AKIRS for a Taxpayer
Identification Number (AISTIN/Tax ID), declare all sources of income from the
business or profession, and file an annual return of income. Taxable income
includes profits or gains from the trade, business, profession or vocation, as
well as other income such as rents, interest, dividends and chargeable gains.
Allowable business expenses, capital allowances and eligible personal deductions
are taken into account in arriving at chargeable income.

## How assessment and payment work

The taxpayer files a return declaring income; AKIRS reviews or raises an
assessment and issues a demand or assessment notice. The taxpayer pays the tax
due through AKIRS's authorised channels. Since February 2026, all state taxes and
levies must be paid through **Akwa Remit** (akwaremit.akwaibomstate.gov.ng).
Taxpayers can register, file and pay online through the IbomTax portal
(ibomtax.net), or visit an AKIRS tax office.

AKIRS's May 2025 public announcement to informal-sector operators listed the
applicable taxes and levies as Personal Income Tax (Direct Assessment for
informal-sector businesses), Economic Development Levy (individual), and
Business Premises Levy.

## Filing deadlines and penalties

Self-employed taxpayers file annual returns of income for each year of
assessment. Late filing or non-payment attracts penalties and interest under the
tax laws, and AKIRS's Enforcement/Recovery Directorate can pursue defaulters.
AKIRS has repeatedly urged business owners to file annual returns of income from
all business sources. Confirm the current filing window for each year with AKIRS
or on the IbomTax portal.

## Paying safely — informal sector

AKIRS's May 2025 public announcement set out important payment guidelines for the
informal sector:

- All payments must be made only through Point-of-Sale (POS) terminals that
  display the official AKIRS logo on the printed receipt.
- Always insist on an official AKIRS receipt for any payment made.
- Do not accept handwritten receipts — they are not valid for informal-sector
  tax compliance.
- Beware of unauthorised individuals or agents requesting payments without
  providing official receipts or issuing fake demand notices. A valid notice must
  include the Director's name and contact number.
- Ensure all payments are made through authorised channels and terminals only.
