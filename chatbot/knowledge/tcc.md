# Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC) — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

A Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC) is an official document issued by the Akwa
Ibom State Internal Revenue Service (AKIRS) confirming that a taxpayer has met
their tax obligations for the relevant period, or is not liable to tax. A TCC is
commonly requested when dealing with government agencies, applying for contracts
or licences, and in other transactions that require proof of tax compliance.
AKIRS both issues TCCs to compliant taxpayers and provides a way to validate that
a presented certificate is genuine.

## Who can apply

Under section 85 of the Personal Income Tax Act (PITA), a taxpayer is entitled to
a TCC where tax assessed for the three years immediately preceding the current
year of assessment has been fully paid — or where no tax was due, or the person
was not liable to tax in any of those three years.

To be eligible, an applicant generally needs:

- A valid Tax Identification Number (TIN) / Taxpayer ID (AISTIN)
- Filed annual (self-assessment or PAYE) returns with AKIRS for each of the
  three preceding years
- All taxes due for those years paid in full, including any penalties or
  interest
- No unresolved assessment queries or disputes with AKIRS

New taxpayers are not excluded. Someone who has been earning or registered for
fewer than three years can still get a TCC — it will show "NIL" or the actual
figures for the years since they became liable to tax, rather than being refused
outright.

## How to apply (step by step)

The general online flow, as used by AKIRS's IbomTax portal (ibomtax.net) and
comparable State IRS eTax platforms, is:

1. **Register / log in** as an individual or company taxpayer on the IbomTax
   portal, obtaining a Taxpayer ID if you do not already have one. Individuals
   use their NIN to verify identity (BVN is not required).
2. **File annual returns** for each of the three years covered, declaring all
   sources of income.
3. **Pay any tax assessed** through the platform's supported payment channels and
   keep the e-receipt or payment confirmation.
4. **Request the TCC / click "Download TCC"** from your taxpayer profile. The
   system runs a checklist confirming that returns have been filed for the three
   years, assessments raised, and taxes paid in full.
5. **Preview the certificate** — check your name, TIN, employer or business
   details, income and tax figures for accuracy.
6. **Download or print** the certificate once all checklist items are cleared.

If any checklist item is outstanding (unfiled return, unpaid assessment,
unresolved query), the platform will block issuance until it is resolved —
either online or by visiting an AKIRS tax office.

## New Tax Clearance Certificate (from October 2024)

In an official announcement, AKIRS stated that it phased out the old tax
clearance certificate and commenced issuance of a new tax clearance certificate
from **1 October 2024**. The new certificate is embellished with more security
features to improve the quality and integrity of the certificate. All tax
clearance certificates issued on or before 30 September 2024 remained valid
until 31 December 2024. Taxpayers should obtain the current certificate rather
than relying on older ones.

## Processing time and validity

- Under section 85(1) PITA, the relevant tax authority is required to issue a TCC
  within two weeks of a complete application, where the taxpayer has paid tax in
  full for the three preceding years (or has no tax due).
- A TCC is not a rolling, open-ended document — it applies to the three-year
  period it references. It is typically requested or regenerated each time a
  fresh transaction requires proof of compliance, and many requesting bodies
  (banks, government agencies) expect a recently issued certificate rather than
  an old one, even though PITA does not fix an "expiry date" separate from the
  three-year period it covers.
- A TCC can be withheld or effectively invalidated if a new assessment or
  dispute arises after issuance and is not resolved.

## How to validate a TCC

- Each TCC carries a unique certificate/reference number and, for the electronic
  version, a QR code.
- A third party (bank, MDA, employer) can scan the QR code with any standard QR
  scanner, which opens a verification page showing the certificate's details as
  held by the tax authority — confirming the TCC is genuine and matches what was
  issued. AKIRS also provides a "Verify Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC)" service
  on its IbomTax portal.
- Where a QR or verification portal is not available, the requesting party can
  contact AKIRS directly to confirm authenticity, as PITA places an obligation on
  requesting agencies and banks to verify TCCs before relying on them.
