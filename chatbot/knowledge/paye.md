# Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

Pay-As-You-Earn (PAYE) is the system by which employers in Akwa Ibom State
deduct personal income tax from the salaries and wages of their employees and
remit it to the Akwa Ibom State Internal Revenue Service (AKIRS). Under PAYE the
employer acts as a collecting agent of government: the employer calculates the
correct deduction for each pay period, deducts it, and remits it to AKIRS on the
employee's behalf. PAYE is an advance payment of the employee's personal income
tax, and the amount deducted is credited against the employee's final tax
liability for the year.

## Who it applies to

PAYE applies to all employers of labour in Akwa Ibom State and to their
employees. AKIRS's FAQ confirms that both temporary and casual workers are
taxable: the Personal Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2011 defines a taxpaying
employee as either a temporary or a permanent employee.

An employee is any person who earns income in the form of salary, wage, fee,
allowance or any other gain or profit from employment, including compensations,
bonuses, premiums, benefits and other perquisites granted by an employer, other
than sums or expenses incurred by the employee in the performance of their
duties from which the employee is not intended to make a profit or gain.

## Rates and reliefs

Employers compute each employee's tax on chargeable income after allowable
reliefs and exemptions. From 1 January 2026 the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 applies:
the first N800,000 of annual income is tax-free, then progressive rates of 15%,
18%, 21%, 23% and 25% apply on higher bands. The Consolidated Relief Allowance
was replaced by a rent relief of the lower of 20% of annual rent or N500,000.
Allowable deductions include pension contributions, National Housing Fund, NHIS,
interest on owner-occupied housing loans, and life insurance or annuity
premiums. For years up to 31 December 2025 the old PITA Sixth Schedule bands
applied (7%, 11%, 15%, 19%, 21%, 24%). See the "Taxes and Rates" topic for the
full band tables.

## Employer registration and remittance

An employer operating in Akwa Ibom State should be registered with AKIRS and
have a Taxpayer Identification Number (AISTIN/Tax ID). The employer deducts PAYE
from each payroll and remits the deductions to AKIRS on a monthly basis. Since
February 2026, all state taxes and levies, including PAYE, must be paid through
**Akwa Remit** (akwaremit.akwaibomstate.gov.ng), the State's approved electronic
payment platform. Employers should also issue employees with evidence of tax
deducted and remitted.

## Annual PAYE returns

In addition to monthly remittances, every employer must file an annual return of
emoluments and tax deducted for the preceding year. AKIRS publishes a public
notice each January inviting employers to file their annual PAYE tax returns —
for example, "Filing of annual employers' tax returns for 2025" was published on
12 January 2026, and the 2024 notice was published in January 2025. Employers
should watch for the annual notice and file within the stated period.

## Deadlines and penalties

PAYE deductions are due monthly, and the annual employer return is filed at the
start of each year as advertised by AKIRS. Late or non-remittance of taxes
attracts penalties and interest under the relevant tax laws. Under the
withholding-tax rules, for example, failure to remit within the stipulated
period attracts a penalty of 10% of the tax not remitted plus interest at the
prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria rate; employers should expect similar
enforcement for PAYE defaults. AKIRS's Enforcement/Recovery Directorate pursues
defaulters, and AKIRS has publicly shut down businesses over non-remittance.

## Employee rights

An employee who wants to confirm whether taxes deducted from their monthly
emoluments have been remitted to the State Government can obtain information
through the Executive Chairman of AKIRS. Employees should keep their payslips
and any evidence of tax deducted, and can request their tax records or a Tax
Clearance Certificate once their tax position is up to date.
