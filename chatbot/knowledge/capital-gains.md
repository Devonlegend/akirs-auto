# Capital Gains Tax — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is tax paid on the disposal of chargeable assets, for
example land. It is charged on the gain that arises when a chargeable asset is
sold or transferred. The gain is generally the difference between what the asset
is disposed of for and its acquisition and related allowable costs. For
individuals liable to tax in Akwa Ibom State, the Akwa Ibom State Internal
Revenue Service (AKIRS) administers the capital gains tax that falls within its
jurisdiction. From 1 January 2026, capital gains for individuals are governed by
the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, which repealed and replaced the Capital Gains Tax Act.

## Who pays

The person who pays capital gains tax is the person who sold the property — that
is, the transferor or lessor. Where assets devolve on account of a deceased
person, the personal representatives or the person on whom the assets devolve
pay the tax.

## Why it is payable

Capital gains tax is a requirement of the law. It applies to the disposal of
chargeable assets by individuals and other persons within AKIRS's jurisdiction.

## Chargeable assets

AKIRS's FAQ lists the following as assets liable to capital gains tax:

- Land
- Buildings and any permanent or semi-permanent structure
- Fixed plant or machinery
- Propelled motor vehicles
- Ships and aircraft

Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, chargeable assets are defined more broadly to
include all types of property, shares, options, rights, debts, digital or
virtual assets, and incorporeal property.

## Exemptions

AKIRS's FAQ lists the following as exempt from paying capital gains tax:

- Government agencies at all levels
- International agencies
- Ecclesiastical, charitable or educational institutions of a public character

Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, additional gains are exempt, including gains from
selling a principal private residence (once in a lifetime, with up to one acre of
land), personal chattels worth N5,000,000 or less, up to two private or
non-commercial motor vehicles per year, assets given or received as gifts (other
than through inheritance), and compensation up to N50,000,000 for personal
injury, professional loss or loss of employment.

## Rate and computation

Capital gains tax is charged on the chargeable gain — the disposal consideration
less the allowable cost of acquiring and improving the asset. Under the Capital
Gains Tax Act, individuals and companies paid a flat 10% on the gain. From
1 January 2026, the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 changed this: capital gains for
individuals are now taxed at the progressive personal income tax rates (0% to
25%) rather than a flat 10%, while companies pay capital gains tax at the
30% company income tax rate. Qualifying small companies are exempt. Confirm the
current treatment and any annual exempt amount with AKIRS.

## How to file and pay

A taxpayer who disposes of a chargeable asset should report the gain and pay the
tax due through AKIRS's authorised channels. Since February 2026, all state
taxes and levies must be paid through **Akwa Remit**
(akwaremit.akwaibomstate.gov.ng). Taxpayers can also register, file and pay
through the IbomTax portal (ibomtax.net) or visit an AKIRS tax office.
