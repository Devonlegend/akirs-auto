# Taxes and Levies AKIRS Administers — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

AKIRS collects taxes, levies and fees that are due to the Akwa Ibom State
Government. The main heads are personal income tax (PAYE and direct assessment),
withholding tax on transactions involving individuals and non-corporate
entities, business premises levy, economic development levy, consumption tax,
stamp duties, capital gains tax on individuals, and taxes on pool betting,
lotteries and gaming. AKIRS also collects various state fees and charges (for
example land use charge and other gazetted revenue heads).

## Personal income tax

Personal income tax is levied on the income of individuals resident in Akwa Ibom
State. It is collected two ways: PAYE (deducted by employers from salaries and
wages) and Direct Assessment (for self-employed persons, traders, professionals
and the informal sector). Employers deduct PAYE monthly and remit it to AKIRS;
self-employed persons file annual returns and pay the tax due.

There are two rate regimes to be aware of, because Nigeria's tax law changed on
1 January 2026.

### Current regime (from 1 January 2026 — Nigeria Tax Act 2025)

The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 replaced the Personal Income Tax Act. It introduced a
new set of progressive bands on chargeable income (income after allowable
deductions and exemptions):

- First N800,000 at 0% (tax-free)
- Next N2,200,000 at 15%
- Next N9,000,000 at 18%
- Next N13,000,000 at 21%
- Next N25,000,000 at 23%
- Above N50,000,000 at 25%

Individuals earning N800,000 or less per year pay no personal income tax. The
Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) was abolished and replaced by a rent relief
of the lower of 20% of annual rent paid or N500,000. Other allowable deductions
include National Housing Fund contributions, National Health Insurance Scheme
contributions, pension contributions, interest on owner-occupied housing loans,
and life insurance or annuity premiums. Gratuity became taxable under the new
law, while pension income from approved pension funds remains exempt.

### Previous regime (PITA, for years up to 31 December 2025)

Under the Personal Income Tax Act, after the Consolidated Relief Allowance and
exemptions, the balance of income was taxed on the Sixth Schedule bands:

- First N300,000 at 7%
- Next N300,000 at 11%
- Next N500,000 at 15%
- Next N500,000 at 19%
- Next N1,600,000 at 21%
- Above N3,200,000 at 24%

The Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) under the amended PITA was the higher of
N200,000 or 1% of gross income, plus 20% of gross income. Items exempt from
emoluments included National Housing Fund contribution, National Health
Insurance Scheme, life assurance premium, National Pension Scheme contributions,
and gratuities.

## Taxes and levies AKIRS collects

AKIRS's official FAQ lists the following among the taxes it collects:

- **Personal Income Tax** from all residents of Akwa Ibom State.
- **Withholding Taxes** on business and economic activities involving a second
  party who is not a corporate entity.
- **Business Premises Levy** from all businesses and companies operating within
  the state.
- **Economic Development Levy (EDL)** from individuals and corporate
  organisations.
- **Consumption Tax**, which replaced the former sales tax.

Other revenue heads AKIRS administers or collects include stamp duties on
state-level instruments, capital gains tax on individuals, pool betting,
lotteries and gaming taxes, and gazetted state fees and charges.

## Business Premises Levy

Businesses and companies operating in Akwa Ibom State pay a Business Premises
Levy. AKIRS's published rates are N10,000 for registration and N5,000 for annual
renewal. Operators should register their business premises with AKIRS and renew
annually. The levy is separate from company income tax and personal income tax.

## Economic Development Levy (EDL)

The Economic Development Levy is payable by individuals at N100 per head per
year. The rates for corporate organisations vary. Payment procedures are
published by AKIRS, and payments must be made through authorised channels with
official AKIRS receipts.

## Consumption Tax

Consumption Tax replaced the former sales tax in Akwa Ibom State. It applies to
the consumption of goods and services within the state and is collected by
AKIRS. The specific rate and scope should be confirmed against the current
AKIRS/state schedule.

## Withholding tax

Withholding tax is an advance payment of income tax deducted at source from
certain payments and remitted to the relevant tax authority. AKIRS handles
withholding tax on transactions involving individuals and non-corporate
entities; the Federal Inland Revenue Service (now the Nigeria Revenue Service)
handles withholding tax on corporate entities. See the "Withholding Tax" topic
for qualifying payments, deadlines and penalties.

## Capital gains tax

Capital gains tax is charged on the gain from disposing of chargeable assets,
for example land and buildings. For individuals liable to tax in Akwa Ibom
State, AKIRS administers the capital gains tax that falls within its
jurisdiction. See the "Capital Gains Tax" topic for details.

## Stamp duties

Stamp duties are charged to confer government and legal recognition on
instruments such as deeds, agreements and leases. Instruments between
individuals, partnerships and enterprises are stamped at the state level, while
instruments between corporate bodies are stamped at the federal level. See the
"Stamp Duties" topic.

## Pool betting, lotteries and gaming

Pool betting, lotteries, gaming and casino taxes are state-level taxes under
the Taxes and Levies (Approved List for Collection) Act. See the "Pool Betting
and Gaming Taxes" topic.

## How payments are made

Following a February 2026 notice, all taxes, levies and revenue payable to the
Akwa Ibom State Government must be paid through **Akwa Remit**
(akwaremit.akwaibomstate.gov.ng), the State's approved electronic payment
platform. The IbomTax portal generates payment references and redirects
taxpayers to Akwa Remit. For informal-sector payments, AKIRS requires payment
through Point-of-Sale (POS) terminals that display the official AKIRS logo, and
taxpayers should always insist on an official AKIRS receipt. Handwritten
receipts are not valid.
