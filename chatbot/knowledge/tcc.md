## Overview

A Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC) is an official document issued by the Akwa
Ibom State Internal Revenue Service (AKIRS) confirming that a taxpayer has met
their tax obligations for the relevant period, or is not liable to tax. A TCC is
commonly requested when dealing with government agencies, applying for contracts
or licences, and in other transactions that require proof of tax compliance.
AKIRS both issues TCCs to compliant taxpayers and provides a way to validate
that a presented certificate is genuine.

## Who can apply

Under Section 85 of the Personal Income Tax Act (PITA), a taxpayer is entitled
to a TCC where tax assessed for the **three years immediately preceding the
current year of assessment** has been fully paid — or where no tax was due, or
the person wasn't liable to tax in any of those three years.

To be eligible, an applicant generally needs:

- A valid Tax Identification Number (TIN) / Taxpayer ID
- Filed annual (self-assessment or PAYE) returns with AKIRS for each of the
  three preceding years
- All taxes due for those years paid in full, including any penalties or
  interest
- No unresolved assessment queries or disputes with AKIRS

New taxpayers aren't excluded. Someone who has been earning/registered for
fewer than three years can still get a TCC — it will show "NIL" or the actual
figures for the years since they became liable to tax, rather than being
refused outright.

## How to apply (step by step)

The general online flow (as used by comparable State IRS eTax platforms, and
by AKIRS's own IbomTax portal) is:

1. **Register / log in** as an individual or company taxpayer on the eTax
   platform, obtaining a Taxpayer ID if you don't already have one (NIN is used
   to verify identity for individuals).
2. **File annual returns** for each of the three years covered, declaring all
   sources of income.
3. **Pay any tax assessed** through the platform's supported payment channels
   and keep the e-receipt/payment confirmation.
4. **Request the TCC / click "Download TCC"** from your taxpayer profile. The
   system runs a checklist confirming: returns filed for the three years,
   assessments raised, and taxes paid in full.
5. **Preview the certificate** — check your name, TIN, employer/business
   details, income, and tax figures for accuracy.
6. **Download or print** the certificate once all checklist items are cleared.

If any checklist item is outstanding (unfiled return, unpaid assessment, unresolved
query), the platform will block issuance until it's resolved — either online or
by visiting an AKIRS tax office.

## Processing time and validity

- Under Section 85(1) PITA, the relevant tax authority is required to issue a
  TCC **within two weeks** of a complete application, where the taxpayer has
  paid tax in full for the three preceding years (or has no tax due).
- A TCC is not a rolling, open-ended document — it applies to the three-year
  period it references. It's typically requested/regenerated each time a fresh
  transaction requires proof of compliance, and many requesting bodies (banks,
  government agencies) expect a recently issued certificate rather than an old
  one, even though PITA itself doesn't fix an "expiry date" separate from the
  three-year period it covers.
- A TCC can be withheld or effectively invalidated if a new assessment or
  dispute arises after issuance and isn't resolved.

## How to validate a TCC

- Each TCC carries a **unique certificate/reference number** and, for the
  electronic version, a **QR code**.
- A third party (bank, MDA, employer) can scan the QR code with any standard
  QR scanner, which opens a verification page showing the certificate's
  details as held by the tax authority — confirming the TCC is genuine and
  matches what was issued.
- Where a QR/verification portal isn't available, the requesting party can
  contact AKIRS directly to confirm authenticity, as PITA places an obligation
  on requesting agencies and banks to verify TCCs before relying on them.
