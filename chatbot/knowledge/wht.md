# Withholding Tax (WHT) — Akwa Ibom State

## Overview

Withholding Tax (WHT) is an advance payment of income tax. It is deducted at
source from certain payments and remitted to the relevant tax authority. The tax
deducted can be set off against any subsequent tax liability payable on that
income by the recipient. In Akwa Ibom State, the Akwa Ibom State Internal
Revenue Service (AKIRS) collects withholding tax that falls within its
jurisdiction — broadly, deductions relating to individuals and non-corporate
entities liable to tax in the state. The Federal Inland Revenue Service (now the
Nigeria Revenue Service) handles withholding tax on corporate entities.

## Who must deduct and remit

Anyone who pays an individual or firm any of the qualifying amounts is a
collecting agent of government and has a duty to deduct the appropriate
withholding tax and remit it to the relevant tax authority (the State Internal
Revenue Service or the Federal Inland Revenue Service). This duty applies to
companies, firms, and government agencies making the relevant payments.

## Payments subject to withholding tax

AKIRS's FAQ lists the following payments on which withholding tax must be
deducted:

- Rent
- Commission
- Management and professional fees
- Consultancy fees
- Technical service fees
- Dividend
- Directors' fees
- Agency arrangements/agreements
- Tenancy agreements
- Supplies

## Currency of deduction

The currency in which tax is deducted and paid to the relevant tax authority is
the currency of the transaction. Where the transaction is in foreign currency,
tax is deducted in the foreign currency and remitted to the relevant tax
authority, which may be paid in local currency at the instance of the revenue
authority.

## Remittance deadlines

Taxes withheld from payments due to corporate bodies and individuals must be
remitted to the relevant authority on the earlier of:

- **30 days for individuals**, and
- **21 days for corporate entities**,

from the date the amount was deducted and from the time the duty to deduct
arose.

## Penalties for default

A person or body corporate that fails to deduct, or having deducted fails to
remit, the tax within the stipulated period is liable to a penalty of 10% of the
tax not deducted or not remitted, in addition to the amount of tax not deducted
or remitted, plus interest at the prevailing Central Bank of Nigeria monetary
rate.

## When withholding tax is a final tax

Withholding tax is a final tax where the income cannot be subjected to further
tax in the hands of the recipient. Under AKIRS's guidance, withholding tax on
dividend, interest, rent and royalties suffered by individuals or corporate
entities not resident in Nigeria represents final tax. In addition, with effect
from January 1996, withholding tax on interest and dividend represents final tax
on those incomes in the hands of individuals resident in Nigeria.

## Withholding tax versus VAT

Withholding tax and Value Added Tax (VAT) are different:

- Withholding tax is paid to the relevant tax authority depending on whether the
  taxpayer is a corporate body or an individual; VAT is paid to the Federal
  Inland Revenue Service under Nigerian tax law.
- VAT is an indirect tax (consumption); withholding tax is a direct tax (income).
- Withholding tax is deducted from income; VAT is added to the invoiced value of
  goods and services.
- A person who suffered withholding tax has the right to set it off against
  future tax liability; VAT cannot be set off.

## Claiming withholding tax credit

To claim withholding tax deducted from your invoice:

- Ensure the collecting agent (the person or entity to whom you rendered
  services) actually remitted the tax deducted to government and obtained a
  certificate of payment issued by AKIRS in your name.
- Include the certificate of payment in your tax return for the relevant year.
- Once your income from all sources has been assessed and tax due ascertained,
  the withholding tax paid at source is set off against the tax due to arrive at
  net tax payable.
- A letter from the collecting agent showing that a taxpayer suffered deduction
  is not enough for AKIRS to grant withholding tax credit, and government
  treasury receipts issued by other government departments showing that they
  deducted tax are likewise not enough to grant tax credit. Only the official
  certificate of payment issued by AKIRS will be accepted.
