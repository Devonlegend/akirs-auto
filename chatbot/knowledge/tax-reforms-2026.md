# Nigeria Tax Reforms 2026 — What Changed for Akwa Ibom Taxpayers

## Overview

Four new tax laws signed on 26 June 2025 took effect on **1 January 2026**:

- Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (NTA)
- Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 (NTAA)
- Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act 2025
- Joint Revenue Board (Establishment) Act 2025

Together they simplify, harmonise and close leakages in Nigeria's tax system.
The NTA repeals and consolidates several older laws, including the Personal
Income Tax Act (PITA), the Companies Income Tax Act (CITA), the Capital Gains
Tax Act, the Value Added Tax Act and the Stamp Duties Act, into a single unified
statute. AKIRS's Executive Chairman, Sir Okon Okon, a member of the Presidential
Committee on Fiscal Policy and Tax Reform, has led sensitisation on the changes
in Akwa Ibom State.

## Personal income tax changes

- The first **N800,000** of annual income is now completely tax-free. This
  covers the N70,000 monthly national minimum wage in full, so low-income
  earners pay no personal income tax.
- New progressive bands apply above that: 15% on the next N2,200,000; 18% on the
  next N9,000,000; 21% on the next N13,000,000; 23% on the next N25,000,000; and
  25% above N50,000,000.
- The **Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) was abolished** and replaced by a
  **rent relief** equal to the lower of 20% of annual rent paid or N500,000.
  Homeowners and those who do not pay rent are not eligible for rent relief.
- Other deductions remain, including pension contributions, National Housing
  Fund, National Health Insurance Scheme, interest on owner-occupied housing
  loans, and life insurance or annuity premiums.
- Gratuity became taxable, while pension income from approved pension funds
  remains exempt.
- The old minimum tax rule (1% of total income) is effectively replaced by the
  expanded 0% band.
- Capital gains for individuals are now taxed at the progressive personal income
  tax rates (up to 25%) instead of the old flat 10% CGT rate; companies pay the
  30% company income tax rate. The CGT Act was repealed.

## Tax Identification Number (Tax ID) mandate

From 1 January 2026, every Nigerian with a valid NIN will have a **Tax ID
automatically generated**. The Tax ID is required when seeking financial
services. AKIRS has clarified that section 8(2) of the Nigeria Tax
Administration Act 2025 does **not** mean bank accounts of citizens without a
Tax ID will be blocked on 1 January 2026 — it simply requires every taxable
Nigerian to provide a Tax ID when seeking financial services. The policy is
intended to ease access to financial services and improve compliance, not to
monitor financial transactions. The Joint Revenue Board (JRB) has issued a press
release on the Tax ID.

## Exemptions and reliefs

The reforms include over 50 exemptions and reliefs aimed at reducing the burden
on citizens and businesses. AKIRS has highlighted in particular:

- A **five-year tax holiday for agricultural enterprises**.
- Exempted items and services including baby products, sanitary products, land
  and buildings, intra-bank transfers, agricultural inputs, healthcare and
  medical services, and educational materials.
- Small companies with gross turnover not exceeding N100 million and total fixed
  assets not exceeding N250 million are exempt from Companies Income Tax,
  Capital Gains Tax and the Development Levy.
- Compensation for loss of employment or injury up to N50,000,000 is exempt.

## Changes to payment and administration in Akwa Ibom

AKIRS announced two important administrative changes in early 2026:

- **NOTICE OF CHANGE IN TAX PAYMENT PROCESSES (13 February 2026):** all taxes,
  levies and revenue payable to the Akwa Ibom State Government must be paid
  through **Akwa Remit** (akwaremit.akwaibomstate.gov.ng), the State's approved
  electronic payment platform. This applies to taxpayers, employers, corporate
  organisations, MDAs, contractors and the general public.
- **Migration To Treasury Single Account (TSA) for IGR (13 February 2026):**
  internally generated revenue collection is being migrated to the State's
  Treasury Single Account.

## What taxpayers should do

- Ensure you have a valid NIN and a Tax ID; register or confirm details on the
  IbomTax portal (ibomtax.net).
- Update payroll calculations to the new bands and rent relief for 2026.
- Keep records of rent paid to support the rent relief claim.
- Make all state tax payments through Akwa Remit and keep official receipts.
- Watch AKIRS's website and the IbomTax portal for filing notices and deadlines.
