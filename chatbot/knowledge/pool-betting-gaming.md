## Overview

Pool Betting and Gaming taxes are levied on operators and activities in the
betting, lottery, and gaming sector within Akwa Ibom State, and are administered
by the Akwa Ibom State Internal Revenue Service (AKIRS) within its jurisdiction.
These taxes generally apply to businesses that run pool betting, lotteries,
casinos, and similar gaming operations in the state. Operators are typically
expected to register, account for the relevant tax on their activities, and
remit it to AKIRS.

## Who it applies to

"Pools betting, lotteries, gaming and casino taxes" are one of the specific
tax heads assigned to **state governments** (not the federal government) under
the Taxes and Levies (Approved List for Collection) Act. In Akwa Ibom, this
covers operators engaged in:

- Pool betting and sports betting (land-based and, where a state licence is
  obtained, online/mobile)
- Lotteries and lotto operations
- Casinos and gaming machines / slot operations
- Related promotional competitions, scratch cards, and similar games of
  chance or skill-and-chance

This is a **state-jurisdiction matter**. Following the Supreme Court's 22
November 2024 ruling (*AG Lagos State & Ors v. AG Federation & Ors*), the
National Lottery Act 2005 is unenforceable outside the Federal Capital
Territory, and states have exclusive constitutional authority to legislate on
and tax gaming activity within their borders. Akwa Ibom is among the states
reported to have maintained its own gaming regulatory posture even before that
ruling, and a **Betting and Lottery Regulatory Agency Bill** has since been
transmitted to the State House of Assembly to formalize a dedicated regulator
for the sector — meaning the institutional split between "who licenses
operators" and "who collects the tax" (AKIRS) may change as that bill
progresses.

It does **not** currently extend to a direct tax on individual bettors'
winnings at the state level — the tax burden falls on operators, not players.

## Rates and basis

TODO (verify with AKIRS): published rates vary significantly by state and are

from other jurisdictions:

- Gaming/lottery tax is typically charged on **Gross Gaming Revenue (GGR)** —
  total stakes wagered minus winnings paid out — rather than on total turnover
- Rates elsewhere range widely: as a rough benchmark, Lagos State charges
  around 2.5% of GGR at state level, versus a 20% GGR-based rate historically
  associated with the (now largely inoperative outside the FCT) federal regime
- Separate from the GGR-based tax, operators may also owe licence/permit fees,
  and — for corporate operators — ordinary Companies Income Tax under CITA
  (as expanded by the Finance Act 2022 and the Nigeria Tax Act 2025) on
  assessable profits, with allowable deductions for winnings paid, agent
  commissions, and taxes/levies already remitted to the regulator

## References

  amended) — assigns "Pools betting, lotteries, gaming and casino taxes" to
  state governments
- Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended) — residual
  legislative powers over gaming reserved to the states (not on the Exclusive
  Legislative List)
- *Attorney General of Lagos State & Ors v. Attorney General of the Federation

  National Lottery Act 2005 unenforceable outside the FCT, confirming state
  exclusivity over gaming regulation and taxation
- Akwa Ibom State Revenue Administration Law, 2016 — establishes AKIRS's
